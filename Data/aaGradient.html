Alveolar–Arterial Oxygen Gradient (A-a Gradient)

Calculation ID: aaGradient
1. Formula & Parameters
A-a Gradient = P_A_O₂ − PaO₂ [where P_A_O₂ = FiO₂ × (Patm − PH₂O) − (PaCO₂ ÷ R)]
Normal Range / Target: Normal Range

Formula Breakdown & Units:

  • FiO₂: Inspired oxygen fraction.
  • PaO₂ & PaCO₂: Arterial blood gas tensions.
2. Clinical & Medical Significance

The Alveolar-Aterial (A-a) oxygen gradient helps differentiate the physiological mechanisms of arterial hypoxemia. A normal A-a gradient indicates pure alveolar hypoventilation or low ambient oxygen, whereas an elevated A-a gradient points directly to intrinsic pulmonary pathology such as V/Q mismatch, diffusion block, or intrapulmonary shunting.

3. Useful Conditions & Medical Decisions

Useful Conditions / Indications:

  • Hypoxemia Workup & Differential Diagnosis

Key Medical Decision: Differentiates extrapulmonary hypoventilation from intrinsic pulmonary parenchymal or vascular disease.

4. Pathophysiological Background

Measures the partial pressure difference between alveolar oxygen and arterial blood oxygen, reflecting gas exchange efficiency.
