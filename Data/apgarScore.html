Apgar Score (Neonatal Transition)

Calculation ID: apgarScore
1. Formula & Parameters
Sum of Appearance, Pulse, Grimace, Activity, and Respiration (0 to 10 points)
Normal Range / Target: 7 – 10 Points (Vigorous Transition)

Formula Breakdown & Units:

  • 5 physiological parameters scored 0, 1, or 2 points each at 1 and 5 minutes post-delivery.
2. Clinical & Medical Significance

The Apgar score is a rapid, standardized method for evaluating the physical condition of a newborn immediately after birth. By assessing heart rate, respiratory effort, muscle tone, reflex irritability, and skin color at 1 and 5 minutes, healthcare providers can immediately identify infants requiring resuscitation and monitor their response to neonatal intervention.

3. Useful Conditions & Medical Decisions

Useful Conditions / Indications:

  • Newborn Transition & Resuscitation
  • Neonatal Intensive Care Triage

Key Medical Decision: Directs immediate neonatal resuscitation (NRP) and ongoing pediatric evaluation.

Score RangePhysiological ConditionNeonatal Action Required
7 – 10 PointsNormal Transition / VigorousRoutine clearing of secretions, drying, warm skin-to-skin contact
4 – 6 PointsModerately Depressed InfantTactile stimulation, clear airway, positive pressure ventilation (PPV) with room air/blended O2
0 – 3 PointsSeverely Depressed InfantImmediate NRP resuscitation: PPV, evaluate HR, chest compressions, vascular access & epinephrine if HR <60 bpm
4. Pathophysiological Background

Reflects cardiorespiratory and neurological adaptation from intrauterine placental gas exchange to autonomous neonatal breathing.
