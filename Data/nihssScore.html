National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS)

Calculation ID: nihssScore
1. Formula & Parameters
Sum of 11 neurological examination categories (0 to 42 points)
Normal Range / Target: 0 Points (Normal Examination)

Formula Breakdown & Units:

  • Evaluates LOC, gaze, visual fields, facial palsy, motor limbs, ataxia, sensory, language, dysarthria, and neglect.
2. Clinical & Medical Significance

The National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) is the universal quantitative standard for evaluating acute ischemic stroke severity. In hyperacute stroke care, NIHSS guides critical time-sensitive therapeutic decisions, including intravenous thrombolysis and endovascular mechanical thrombectomy. A score of 6 or higher combined with symptoms of large vessel occlusion strongly identifies candidates for emergent neurointerventional reperfusion therapy.

3. Useful Conditions & Medical Decisions

Useful Conditions / Indications:

  • Acute Ischemic Stroke
  • Transient Ischemic Attack (TIA)
  • Intracranial Hemorrhage Neurological Staging

Key Medical Decision: Determines eligibility for intravenous alteplase/tenecteplase (<4.5 hours) and mechanical thrombectomy (<24 hours).

4. Pathophysiological Background

Reflects the volume and specific neuroanatomical localization of cerebral ischemia within vascular territories (e.g., MCA, ACA, PCA).
