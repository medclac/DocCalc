Perineal Tear Classification (Sultan / RCOG)

Calculation ID: perinealTearClassification
1. Formula & Parameters
Anatomical staging of obstetric perineal trauma (1st to 4th Degree / OASIS)
Normal Range / Target: Normal Range

Formula Breakdown & Units:

  • Classifies tears from superficial skin/mucosa (1st) to complete anal sphincter and rectal mucosal breach (4th).
2. Clinical & Medical Significance

The Sultan classification provides a standardized anatomical grading system for obstetric perineal trauma, specifically identifying Obstetric Anal Sphincter Injuries (OASIS, Grades 3 and 4). Accurate classification and meticulous surgical repair in an operating theatre are essential to prevent long-term fecal incontinence and dyspareunia.

3. Useful Conditions & Medical Decisions

Useful Conditions / Indications:

  • Obstetric Perineal Trauma & Episiotomy Repair
  • Anal Incontinence Prevention

Key Medical Decision: Guides surgical repair setting (delivery room vs operating theatre) and postoperative antibiotic/laxative regimens.

2nd DegreePerineal muscles and perineal body (sphincter intact)Delivery bed under adequate local anesthesiaAbsorbable continuous suture (Vicryl Rapide), paracetamol/NSAIDs
3rd Degree (3a / 3b)External Anal Sphincter (<50% or ≥50% torn)Operating theatre under regional or general anesthesiaEnd-to-end or overlap EAS repair with PDS, IV antibiotics, lactulose for 10 days
3rd Degree (3c) / 4th DegreeBoth EAS & IAS torn ± anal mucosa breached (OASIS)Operating theatre with senior obstetricianInterrupted 3-0 PDS for IAS, submucosal repair of anorectal mucosa, broad-spectrum antibiotics, laxatives, pelvic floor physio follow-up
4. Pathophysiological Background

Mechanical stretching and tearing of perineal body muscles, bulbospongiosus, and external/internal anal sphincters during fetal head crowning.
