Pediatric Assessment Triangle (PAT)

Calculation ID: pediatricAssessmentTriangle
1. Formula & Parameters
PAT = Appearance (TICLS) + Work of Breathing + Circulation to Skin
Normal Range / Target: All 3 components Normal = Stable Child

Formula Breakdown & Units:

  • Appearance (TICLS): Tone, Interactivity, Consolability, Look/gaze, Speech/cry.
  • Work of Breathing: Retractions, nasal flaring, grunting, stridor, wheezing.
  • Circulation to Skin: Pallor, mottling, cyanosis, petechiae.
2. Clinical & Medical Significance

Rapidly differentiates physiological stability from life-threatening cardiorespiratory failure or shock before invasive procedures or touching the patient, preventing sudden arrest.

3. Useful Conditions & Medical Decisions

Useful Conditions / Indications:

  • Respiratory Distress vs Failure
  • Compensated vs Decompensated Shock
  • Primary CNS Dysfunction / Metabolic Crisis
  • Cardiopulmonary Failure

Key Medical Decision: Determines immediate resuscitation tier (PALS algorithm activation, BVM ventilation, fluid bolus 10-20 mL/kg, or routine secondary survey).

4. Pathophysiological Background

Acute pediatric physiological decompensation typically manifests first as increased work of breathing (compensatory tachypnea/retractions), followed by circulatory vasoconstriction (compensated shock), and finally central nervous system hypoxemia/hypoperfusion resulting in lethargy, hypotonia, and cardiovascular collapse.

5. Medical Background & Evidence

First introduced by the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) and advanced pediatric life support educators (Dieckmann et al., 2010) as a universal rapid initial impression tool.
