Acute Asthma Severity Assessment (Adult BTS/SIGN)

Calculation ID: asthmaAdult
1. Formula & Parameters
Emergency clinical stratification into Moderate, Severe, or Life-Threatening acute asthma
Normal Range / Target: Normal Range

Formula Breakdown & Units:

  • Evaluates PEF percentage, SpO₂, respiratory rate, heart rate, speech capability, and life-threatening features.
2. Clinical & Medical Significance

Rapidly grading acute asthma severity per British Thoracic Society (BTS/SIGN) guidelines ensures emergency clinicians immediately recognize life-threatening exacerbations (silent chest, cyanosis, exhaustion, PEF <33%) and administer aggressive rescue therapy, magnesium sulfate, and critical care support.

3. Useful Conditions & Medical Decisions

Useful Conditions / Indications:

  • Acute Asthma Exacerbation Triage
  • Emergency Department Bronchospasm Management

Key Medical Decision: Triggers immediate life-saving interventions including high-flow oxygen, nebulized bronchodilators, IV magnesium, and ICU referral.

Severity TierClinical Diagnostic CriteriaImmediate Emergency Action
Moderate AsthmaPEF 50–75%, SpO₂ ≥92%, RR <25, HR <110, full sentencesNebulized SABA (salbutamol 5mg) + oral prednisolone 40–50 mg
Severe AsthmaPEF 33–50%, RR ≥25, HR ≥110, or speaks in phrases onlyHigh-flow O₂, back-to-back SABA + ipratropium nebulizers, systemic steroids, urgent senior review
LIFE-THREATENINGPEF <33%, SpO₂ <92%, silent chest, cyanosis, confusion, exhaustionIMMEDIATE CRITICAL CARE CALL: IV Magnesium sulfate (2g), prepare for emergency intubation, ICU transfer
4. Pathophysiological Background

Acute airway smooth muscle bronchospasm, mucosal edema, and thick mucous plugging leading to severe expiratory airflow obstruction.

5. Medical Background & Evidence

British Thoracic Society; Scottish Intercollegiate Guidelines Network (BTS/SIGN. Thorax. 2019;74(Suppl 1):1-140).
