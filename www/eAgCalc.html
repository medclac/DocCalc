HbA1c to Estimated Average Glucose (eAG)

Calculation ID: eAgCalc
1. Formula & Parameters
eAG (mg/dL) = 28.7 × HbA1c − 46.7 | eAG (mmol/L) = eAG (mg/dL) ÷ 18.018
Normal Range / Target: < 5.7% (< 117 mg/dL)

Formula Breakdown & Units:

  • HbA1c (%): Glycated hemoglobin percentage.
2. Clinical & Medical Significance

Translating laboratory HbA1c into estimated average glucose (eAG) is a critical clinical communication tool in diabetes management [cite: Nathan DM et al., 2008]. Patients frequently struggle to conceptualize percentage-based A1c values in comparison to their daily home glucometer or continuous glucose monitor (CGM) fingerstick readings. Providing a concrete average glucose value bridges the gap between chronic glycemic integration and daily self-management trends. This translation empowers patients and clinicians to identify discordances between point-in-time fingersticks and long-term glycation, evaluates chronic glucotoxicity, and refines lifestyle or pharmacological regimens to reduce microvascular and macrovascular complications [cite: Nathan DM et al., 2008].

3. Useful Conditions & Medical Decisions

Useful Conditions / Indications:

  • Type 1 and Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus [cite: Nathan DM et al., 2008]
  • Prediabetes Surveillance [cite: Nathan DM et al., 2008]
  • Gestational Diabetes Glycemic Monitoring [cite: Nathan DM et al., 2008]

Key Medical Decision: Guides intensification or de-escalation of antihyperglycemic pharmacotherapy to achieve personalized ADA A1c targets (typically <7.0% for non-pregnant adults) [cite: Nathan DM et al., 2008].

4. Pathophysiological Background

Glucose molecules non-enzymatically attach to the N-terminal valine of the hemoglobin beta chain in a slow, irreversible, concentration-dependent reaction throughout the 120-day lifespan of circulating erythrocytes [cite: Nathan DM et al., 2008]. The ADAG trial established a robust linear regression model proving that cumulative plasma glucose exposure directly dictates glycation density, allowing accurate conversion between integrated percentages and average daily glycemia [cite: Nathan DM et al., 2008].
